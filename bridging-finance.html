What is bridging finance?

Bridging finance is generally a form of short-term finance that can be used when funding is needed for a limited period before longer-term finance or another source of funds becomes available.

Bridging finance is commonly associated with property transactions, although the exact purposes accepted will depend on the relevant finance provider and its criteria.

Important: Bridging finance can be relatively short-term and may involve fees and interest. You should carefully consider the total cost and exit arrangements before proceeding.

How does bridging finance work?

A bridging finance provider may provide funds for a specified period, with the expectation that the borrowing will be repaid through an agreed exit strategy.

The exact structure, interest rate, fees, security requirements and repayment arrangements vary between providers.

What can bridging finance be used for?

Depending on the provider and circumstances, bridging finance may be considered for purposes such as:

  • Property purchases
  • Property refurbishment
  • Buying a property before selling another
  • Property development projects
  • Other short-term funding requirements

What is an exit strategy?

An exit strategy is the planned method for repaying bridging finance. This is an important part of considering any bridging arrangement.

Depending on the circumstances, an exit strategy might involve selling a property, refinancing onto longer-term finance or using another source of funds accepted by the provider.

What should you consider before taking bridging finance?

Bridging finance can be useful in certain circumstances, but it can also be more complex and costly than some longer-term finance arrangements.

  • The interest rate and how interest is charged
  • Arrangement and other fees
  • The maximum term of the finance
  • The security required
  • The planned exit strategy
  • Whether you can afford the finance
  • The consequences if the exit strategy does not work

Bridging finance and your circumstances

Providers may consider factors including the property or asset involved, the amount requested, the proposed term, the intended use of the funds and the proposed repayment or exit strategy.

There is no guarantee that bridging finance will be available, that an application will be approved or that any particular rate or amount will be offered.
