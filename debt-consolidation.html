What is debt consolidation?

Debt consolidation generally involves combining some existing borrowing into another finance arrangement. The aim may be to make repayments easier to manage or restructure existing borrowing.

Whether debt consolidation is suitable depends on your individual circumstances, the debts involved and the terms available from the relevant finance provider.

Important: Debt consolidation does not automatically reduce the amount you owe. A longer repayment period can sometimes mean paying more overall, even if individual payments are lower.

How does debt consolidation work?

If a suitable consolidation product is available, existing debts may be repaid using the new finance arrangement. You would then make repayments according to the terms of the new agreement.

The exact process depends on the finance provider and the type of borrowing involved.

Why do people consider debt consolidation?

People may consider debt consolidation for different reasons, including wanting to simplify several repayments or explore whether a different repayment arrangement could be appropriate.

  • Managing multiple existing debts
  • Reducing the number of separate monthly payments
  • Exploring alternative repayment arrangements
  • Reviewing the overall cost of existing borrowing

What should you consider before consolidating debt?

Debt consolidation is not suitable for everyone. Before proceeding, it is important to understand the full cost and terms of any proposed finance.

  • The total amount repayable
  • The interest rate
  • Arrangement or other fees
  • The length of the new agreement
  • Early repayment charges on existing borrowing
  • Whether the new repayments are affordable
  • Whether the new arrangement is secured against an asset

Can debt consolidation help with monthly payments?

In some circumstances, a consolidation arrangement may result in a lower monthly payment. However, this can depend on the interest rate and repayment period, and a lower monthly payment does not necessarily mean a lower overall cost.

Debt consolidation and your circumstances

Providers may consider factors such as your existing borrowing, income, affordability, credit history and their own eligibility criteria.

There is no guarantee that finance will be available, that an application will be approved or that consolidation will be cheaper than your existing borrowing.
