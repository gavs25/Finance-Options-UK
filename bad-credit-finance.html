Can I get finance with bad credit?

Having a poor or damaged credit history does not necessarily mean that every finance option will be unavailable. Different lenders have different eligibility criteria and may consider information such as your income, affordability, existing commitments and credit history.

However, approval is never guaranteed. A lender or finance provider will decide whether an application is suitable based on its own assessment and criteria.

What is bad credit?

Bad credit is a general term often used when someone has a history of missed payments, defaults, County Court Judgments, high levels of existing borrowing or other information that may make obtaining credit more difficult.

Your credit history is only one part of the picture. Lenders may also consider your current financial circumstances and whether repayments appear affordable.

Finance options to consider

Personal finance

Personal finance may be available to some applicants depending on their circumstances, affordability and the lender's eligibility requirements.

Debt consolidation

Debt consolidation involves replacing multiple debts with another borrowing arrangement. It can have advantages and disadvantages, so the total cost and repayment terms should be considered carefully.

Vehicle finance

Some vehicle finance providers consider applicants with a range of credit histories. The available terms can vary significantly depending on individual circumstances.

What lenders may consider

  • Your income and employment circumstances
  • Your existing financial commitments
  • Your credit history and previous repayment behaviour
  • Your affordability
  • The amount you want to borrow
  • The repayment period
  • The lender's own eligibility criteria
Important: No legitimate finance service should promise that you will automatically be accepted. Any application remains subject to the relevant provider's checks and criteria.

Before applying for finance

Consider whether borrowing is affordable and whether you are comfortable with the repayments. Look at the total amount repayable, interest rate, fees and length of the agreement rather than focusing only on the monthly payment.

If you are already struggling with debt, getting independent debt advice before taking on further borrowing may be appropriate.
